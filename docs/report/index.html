What reinforcement learning adds to a vision-language model

Guangbo YuQwen3-VL 2B and 8BCode and records
Abstract Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has become a standard step after supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for language models, but for vision-language models it is not clear when it adds anything beyond SFT on the same data. We post-train Qwen3-VL at 2B and 8B with SFT, with GRPO and a 0/1 correctness reward, and with SFT followed by GRPO, and score every model on fixed test sets under a short-answer and a step-by-step prompt, through an evaluation harness that separates real gains from answer format, coverage from sharpening, and image use from answer priors. On chart questions, where reasoning pays, GRPO keeps its reasoning and raises accuracy from 69.7% to 80.1% (77.2% to 82.6% under an audited judge), about ten points above every short-answer model. On medical images, GRPO after SFT is the best model in all three settings tested. RL's gains need the image: with a black image in its place, RL answers no more questions than the base model in five of six settings, it moves the model five to sixteen times less than SFT on medical data, and on data it never saw it keeps a lead of 0.8 to 2.3 points over SFT. Applied to two 3D CT benchmarks, the same harness finds that most of their published accuracy is reachable without the scan; with those shortcuts removed, RL and SFT reach the same accuracy.

1Introduction

Reinforcement learning with a verifiable reward trains a model on its own samples and pays it only when a program confirms the answer. For language models this is the step that turns a fine-tuned model into a reasoning one. Vision-language models answer a different kind of question: most visual questions are answered by looking rather than deriving, and a reward that checks only the final answer can just as well teach a model to stop thinking. Whether RL adds anything beyond supervised fine-tuning on the same images therefore depends on the data, and the comparisons that would settle it are rarely run under one recipe, one evaluation and matched training data.

We run those comparisons. Qwen3-VL at 2B and 8B is trained with SFT, with GRPO and a 0/1 correctness reward, and with SFT followed by GRPO, on photo questions (VQAv2, GQA), chart questions (ChartQA) and medical images (SLAKE, OmniMedVQA). A dataset enters training only after a screen confirms that its image matters and that RL will receive a learning signal, and every model is scored by an evaluation harness built to tell real gains from answer format and image use from answer priors. The same harness, pointed at two 3D CT benchmarks, shows how much of their published accuracy is reachable without the image.

The contributions are:

  • Where reasoning pays, RL keeps it and wins: 69.7% → 80.1% on ChartQA, 10.4 points above the best short-answer model, with sampling coverage at pass@32 kept.
  • RL after SFT gives the best medical model in all three settings tested at 2B and 8B, and RL alone loses less than SFT on medical data it never saw.
  • RL's gains are grounded in the image, while SFT also learns answers it can give without looking; on medical data RL changes the model five to sixteen times less.
  • An audit of two 3D CT benchmarks: text-only rules and models trained on black images reach most of the published accuracy, a debiased version removes the shortcuts, and on it RL matches SFT.
  • A nine-layer evaluation harness (Section 3) that every number in this report passes through.

2Method

2.1Models and training

All models start from Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct or Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct and train LoRA adapters of rank 16 on the language model's attention and MLP layers in bf16; the vision encoder stays frozen. Three training methods are compared on the same training questions.

MethodSignalRecipe
SFTReference short answers, loss on answer tokensOne epoch; learning rate 1e-4 (2B), 5e-5 (8B)
GRPO1 if the tagged answer matches the reference after normalization, else 04 questions × 8 samples per update at temperature 1; group-relative advantages; token-level loss; no KL term; learning rate 1e-5 decaying to 0; rollouts under a step-by-step or a short-answer prompt
SFT → GRPOBoth, in sequenceGRPO started from the SFT model, same updates

Rollouts come from vLLM serving the trained adapter unmerged, so the sampler is exactly the policy being trained; merging into bf16 weights rounds small updates away and made the sampler lag the policy by up to 0.2 nats per token. An importance-sampling correction capped at 3 absorbs the remaining difference. One RL epoch draws each training question once, the exposure SFT has.

2.3Choosing data where RL can learn

Each candidate dataset passes a screen on 500 training questions with the 2B base model before any training. Its gates were written before the candidates ran: replacing the image with a black or a swapped one must cost at least 10 points, at least 25% of questions must be learnable (some but not all of 8 samples correct, so GRPO receives a gradient), answers must be parseable, and accuracy must not already sit at the ceiling. Whether reasoning helps is measured with the same tagged final answer in both prompts, so the difference isolates the reasoning from the answer format. ChartQA, SLAKE and OmniMedVQA passed beside the photo baseline; the full screen is in Appendix A10.

4Results

4.1Where reasoning pays, RL keeps it and beats SFT by ten points

ChartQA's human-written questions are the dataset where the screen found reasoning helpful: numeric questions gain 5.6 points from it. Trained with step-by-step rollouts for one epoch, GRPO keeps its reasoning (205 → 162 tokens) and raises accuracy from 69.7% to 80.1% (+10.4 [+8.2, +12.6] points). SFT on the same questions reaches 66.2% under the reasoning prompt and 68.8% with short answers, so the reasoning-trained model ends 10.4 points above the best short-answer model. Started from SFT, GRPO regrows the reasoning SFT had shortened (76 → 197 tokens) and adds 13.5 points over SFT.

The audited judge keeps half of the gain, 77.2% → 82.6% (+5.4 [+3.5, +7.4]), because many of the base model's misses are right answers in the wrong form, such as "20%" for a label of 20. Under the audit the reasoning-trained model still leads the best short-answer model by 11.7 points.

Trained with reasoning, RL sharpens without narrowing. Sampled 32 times per question, GRPO raises pass@1 from 66.4% to 79.8% and ends level with the base model at pass@32 (91.0% → 91.5%). With short-answer rollouts on the same data, GRPO gains at pass@1 (62.7% → 67.8%) but loses coverage at pass@32 (86.1% → 77.1%).

4.2After SFT, RL gives the best medical model

GRPO started from the SFT model is the best model on SLAKE at 2B (74.8% → 76.2%) and 8B (77.2% → 78.2%) and on OmniMedVQA collections never seen in training (85.0% → 85.6%). SFT teaches the domain's answers; RL then sharpens them on the model's own samples. GRPO alone gains less in domain but loses less on PathVQA, a medical set no model trained on: every SFT model falls below its base model there, while GRPO stays within 1.8 points of it.

4.3RL's gains come from the image

Replacing each image with a black one shows what a model answers without looking. RL from the base model answers as many questions blind as the base model in five of six settings (changes of −0.3 to +0.5 points, every interval including zero) and 3.2 more on the held-out OmniMedVQA collections. SFT answers more without the image in every setting it trained in, up to 15.4 points on the held-out collections with the same training questions as RL. At 8B, 10.4 of SFT's 19.1-point gain on SLAKE survives a black image, against 0.4 of RL's 10.3. RL improves the answers that depend on the image; SFT also learns which answers are likely for a question.

On medical data RL also moves the model far less. The forward KL from the base model, measured on the base model's own samples for the task's questions, is five to sixteen times smaller for GRPO than for SFT. RL's Razor (Shenfeld et al., 2025) finds that forgetting tracks this distance, and the medical results agree: GRPO, which moves least, loses less than SFT on PathVQA in every setting.

4.4On data it never trained on, RL keeps an edge over SFT

Trained on VQAv2 and GQA with short answers, GRPO scores 81.0% on TextVQA against SFT's 80.1% (+0.9 [+0.2, +1.6]) and 43.6% on VizWiz against 42.5% (+1.0 [+0.5, +1.6]). SFT trained on exactly the 4,000 questions GRPO drew does not close the gap (80.1% on TextVQA, 42.4% on VizWiz), so the edge is not a matter of how many questions each method saw. On medical data the same holds for PathVQA, where GRPO leads SFT by 0.8 to 2.3 points across the three training settings (Table 1).

4.5A reasoning habit learned on charts carries over to photos

The ChartQA-trained GRPO model, scored on photo questions it never trained on, keeps a shorter chain of reasoning and gains 15 to 16 points on VQAv2 and GQA under the reasoning prompt (6 on TextVQA), while its short-answer accuracy moves by at most 0.3 points. What RL teaches here is a response habit, and the habit transfers across domains.

5Do 3D CT benchmarks need the scan?

Benchmarks for 3D medical vision-language models report steady gains from fine-tuning. The same harness asks how much of that accuracy is reachable without looking: a text-only rule that sees the question and options, a model trained and tested on black images, and the same recipe with the image. Two widely used CT benchmarks are measured: DeepTumorVQA v2 (abdominal CT, multiple choice and free form, 2D views of each scan) and M3D-VQA (CT and MRI, multiple choice, questions only).

On DeepTumorVQA the shortcut lives in the answer options and the answer frequencies. Distractors for a measured value are drawn from 0.7 to 1.3 times the true value, so the true value is the second or third smallest of four options 88% of the time, and binary and categorical answers follow the patients' prevalence (85% "No" for colon lesions). A rule that uses only these patterns scores 60.7% against 34.5% chance. Free form removes the options but not the frequencies: the median training answer per question type and organ scores 57.4%, a model trained on black images 57.6%, and the same recipe with the scans 60.3% (+2.7 [+2.1, +3.3] points for the image, almost all of it on lesion existence).

M3D-VQA's shortcut is position and phrasing: option A is correct in 47.5% of its test questions, and the most common letter per question type alone scores 50.6%. Trained on black images, a 2B model comes within 2.3 points of the published 3D model on the three clinical question types (organ, abnormality, location).

5.1A debiased DeepTumorVQA, and RL on it

A debiased version removes both shortcuts: every measured value gets options with the true value at a uniformly random rank and a spacing that does not reveal it, counts get a window of consecutive integers, every option list is shuffled, and each discrete question type keeps equally many questions per answer. On the remaining 6,607 test questions the text-only rule falls to 35.2% against 33.7% chance, and a model trained on the original options falls from 61.4% to 36.0%.

Without the shortcuts, little is left for a 2B model to learn from 2D views of the scan. GRPO matches SFT (−0.3 [−1.6, +1.0] from the base model, +0.7 [−0.4, +1.8] after SFT), every model's one visual skill is lesion existence, measurement stays at chance, and 32 slices of the CT video do no better than nine. The benchmark's measurement and reasoning questions call for inputs that carry the volume, such as 3D encoders or tools that measure it.

6Discussion

The reward prices what it can verify. A 0/1 correctness reward keeps whatever raises the chance of a correct final answer. On charts, reasoning does, and RL keeps and refines it; on photo questions answered by looking, it does not, and the same recipe shortens responses to a few tokens (Appendix A1). The choice of data decides what RL learns, which is why datasets enter training here only after a screen measures the reasoning payoff under matched answer format.

RL is a conservative fine-tuner. Its gains need the image, it moves the model little where SFT moves it far, and it loses less on unseen data. SFT is the stronger teacher of a new domain's answers, including answers that follow from the question alone. The two combine well: SFT first, then RL, gives the best medical model in every setting tested.

A benchmark has to need the image before it can measure looking. When options and answer frequencies carry the answer, fine-tuning gains say little about vision, and RL rewarded on those questions mostly learns the shortcut. Checking text-only and black-image baselines before training is cheap, and the debiasing recipe here applies to any benchmark whose questions are generated from masks or templates.

7Future work

  1. RL on the image-grounded question types. Lesion existence is the one skill a 2B model learns from 2D views of the debiased CT questions; RL against SFT restricted to it is a clean test of whether RL adds visual skill when the image is what pays.
  2. Volumetric perception as tools. Measurement and reasoning questions call for the volume: a 3D foundation model or slice-level visual features that locate organs and lesions, returned to the language model as evidence it can inspect rather than as answers, evaluated on the debiased benchmark where shortcuts no longer score.
  3. A second domain where reasoning pays, such as diagrams, geometry or documents, through the same screen, and the chart result at 8B.
  4. Calibrated RL. RL raises a model's agreement with itself faster than its accuracy (Appendix A5); rewarding abstention when unsure targets this directly.
  5. Learning when to reason. One model trained on photos and charts together lost its reasoning everywhere (Appendix A6); rollout prompts conditioned on the question type are the next design to test.

ANegative and null results

Results that set the limits of the findings above, each with its numbers. They use the same recipe, data and evaluation.

A1
On photo questions RL deletes the reasoning, and most of its exact-match gain is answer form.

VQAv2, 2B, reasoning prompt: 55.1% (148 tokens) → 78.3% (8 tokens) after GRPO, against 77.9% for the base model with short answers. Under the audited judge: 77.7% → 82.1%.

A2
In domain, short-answer RL reaches SFT's level and no further.

2B short answers, VQAv2 / GQA: base 77.9 / 59.5, GRPO with 4,000 questions 78.6 / 60.3, GRPO on all 55,438 questions 79.8 / 62.4, SFT 79.9 / 62.3.

A3
On photo questions RL narrows what the model can sample.

pass@32 with short answers, VQAv2 / GQA: base 79.8 / 82.7, GRPO 76.6 / 75.1 (full pool 74.0 / 71.9), SFT 82.4 / 87.3, while GRPO leads at pass@1 (62.9 → 65.9 on VQAv2).

A4
On medical images, SFT beats RL from the base model in domain.

SLAKE: SFT 74.8 vs GRPO 65.6 (2B), 77.2 vs 68.3 (8B). Held-out OmniMedVQA collections, same 4,916 training questions: SFT 83.1 vs GRPO 79.1.

A5
RL is overconfident.

On SLAKE at 2B, GRPO's majority answer is shared by 89.4% of its samples while it is right 65.7% of the time. Expected calibration error in short-answer settings: GRPO 12.1–23.7 points, SFT 2.2–8.6 (SLAKE 23.7 vs 2.2; base model 24.7).

A6
One model trained on photos and charts does not learn when to reason.

After 1,750 updates of chart reasoning it collapsed to 11-token answers everywhere and ends at 65.9% on ChartQA against 80.1% for the chart-trained model; the photo-trained model scores 63.0% there (10 tokens).

A7
Training settings did not move results.

On photos, a filtered pool, refilled dead groups, four times the batch and three times the learning rate stay within 0.35 points. On ChartQA, 8 single-setting changes (loss aggregation, KL penalty, reward scaling, response cap, list-aware reward, canvas size, image geometry) read −1.5 to +1.0 points against a same-seed repeat of 0.5, all inside their intervals.

A8
Vision-side options did not improve SLAKE.

2B SLAKE, short answers: SFT 74.8, with LoRA on the vision encoder 75.1, on upscaled images 74.1; GRPO 65.6, on upscaled images 66.0, with a masked-image perception loss 56.0. The perception loss taught the model to reply that the image is missing: 3.7% correct with a black image.

A9
RL moves the model less than SFT only where SFT moves it far.

Forward KL from the base model: VQAv2 SFT 0.14 vs GRPO 0.23 nats; short-answer ChartQA 0.39 vs 1.02.

A10
Datasets rejected at screening.

Images changed the answers by under 10 points on Kvasir-VQA-x1, PathVQA, ReXVQA, MedThinkVQA and DeepTumorVQA (audited separately in Section 5); CLEVR counting and compositional CLEVR are near the ceiling.

Scope

  • One training seed per model; intervals cover the sampling of test questions.
  • The judge audit, pass@k, the unseen sets and the CT audit are at 2B; the main photo matrix and SLAKE also at 8B.
  • Fixed test subsets scored locally, not benchmark evaluation servers; contamination is checked with exact image hashes against this project's training pools.

Reproducibility

Training uses TRL 1.8 with PEFT and colocated vLLM; evaluation runs through one protocol-hashed harness that stores per-question predictions for every model and protocol. Experiments are declared in experiments.toml before they run, and this page is rebuilt from the records with python -m scripts.report_page. About 279 GPU-hours in total, on cluster GPUs (RTX PRO 6000 96 GB, A100 80 GB, A30 24 GB) and one RTX 4090 24 GB; the 2B pipeline runs end to end on a single 24 GB GPU.

Terms

GRPO
Group Relative Policy Optimization: sample several responses per question and use each one's reward minus the group mean as its advantage, so no value network is needed.
Verifiable reward
A reward computed by a program, here 1 when the tagged answer matches the reference after normalization and 0 otherwise.
Audited judge
Exact-match hits plus LLM-judge acceptances, each acceptance weighted by the share of its stratum that an image-aware auditor confirmed.
pass@k
The probability that at least one of k samples is correct, estimated without bias from 32 samples.
Forward KL
E[log p₀(y|x) − log p(y|x)] over responses y sampled from the starting model p₀: how much less likely the trained model p finds what the start would say.
Calibration error
The gap between confidence (share of samples giving the majority answer) and accuracy, averaged over 10 confidence bins.
