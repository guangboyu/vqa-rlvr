What reinforcement learning adds to a vision-language model

Guangbo YuQwen3-VL 2B and 8BCode and records
Abstract When does reinforcement learning with a verifiable reward improve a vision-language model beyond supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the same data? We train Qwen3-VL at 2B and 8B with SFT, with GRPO and a 0/1 correctness reward, and with SFT followed by GRPO, and score every model through one evaluation harness. The answer depends on what the task pays for. RL mostly sharpens what the model can already sample. Where reasoning pays, it wins: on ChartQA it raises accuracy from {{cq.base.r}}% to {{cq.grpo.r}}%, {{cq.oversft}} points above SFT's best. Where reasoning does not pay, it changes the form of the answer and not the ability behind it: on VQAv2, reasoning answers shrink from {{ph.vqav2.base.tok}} to {{ph.vqav2.grpor.tok}} tokens and end at the level of short answers. Where the answer is out of reach, SFT has to go first, and RL after SFT is the best model. RL's gains also come from the image: at 8B on SLAKE, {{ba.slk8.sft.blackgain}} of SFT's {{ba.slk8.sft.gain}}-point gain survives a black image, against {{ba.slk8.grpo.blackgain}} of RL's {{ba.slk8.grpo.gain}}.

1The claim

RL with a verifiable reward trains a model on its own samples and pays it only when a program confirms the answer. For language models this step turns a fine-tuned model into a reasoning one. Vision-language models answer a different mix of questions: many are settled by looking rather than deriving, and a reward that checks only the final answer can just as well teach a model to stop thinking. The comparisons that would show when RL helps are rarely run under one recipe, one evaluation and matched data. We run them.

Whether RL beats SFT depends on what the task pays for. RL mostly sharpens what the model can already sample; where the answer is out of reach, SFT has to go first. The rest of this report hangs from that claim, and each branch links to the page with all of its evidence.

2Setup

All models start from Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct or Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct and train LoRA adapters of rank 16 on the language model's attention and MLP layers in bf16; the vision encoder stays frozen. Three methods are compared on the same training questions.

Each candidate dataset passes a screen on 500 training questions before any training, with gates written before the candidates ran: a black or swapped image must cost at least 10 points, at least 25% of questions must give GRPO a gradient, answers must be parseable, and accuracy must not already sit at the ceiling (Table A1).

3Where reasoning pays, RL wins and keeps it

Trained with step-by-step rollouts for one epoch on ChartQA's human-written questions, GRPO raises accuracy from {{cq.base.r}}% to {{cq.grpo.r}}% ({{cq.gain.ci}} points) and keeps its reasoning ({{cq.base.tok}} → {{cq.grpo.tok}} tokens). SFT on the same questions reaches {{cq.sft.s}}% at best, so GRPO ends {{cq.oversft}} points above it. The audited judge credits many of Instruct's misses that are right answers in the wrong form, such as "20%" for a label of 20; under it the gain is {{cq.audgain}} points ({{cq.base.aud}}% → {{cq.grpo.aud}}%).

The answers were already within reach. Sampled 32 times per question, GRPO raises pass@1 from {{pk.base.r.1}}% to {{pk.grpo.1}}% and ends level with Instruct at pass@32 ({{pk.base.r.32}}% → {{pk.grpo.32}}%): it learns to choose what Instruct could already sample. With short-answer rollouts on the same data, it gains at pass@1 and gives up coverage at pass@32 ({{pk.base.s.32}}% → {{pk.short.32}}%).

Scope: one dataset so far, and SFT here trains on short answers. A second chart-and-document source and SFT on reasoning traces are the next tests.

4Where it doesn't, RL changes form, not ability

A 0/1 correctness reward keeps whatever raises the chance of a correct final answer. On photo questions, reasoning lowers it. On VQAv2 under the reasoning prompt, GRPO goes from {{ph.vqav2.base.r}}% at {{ph.vqav2.base.tok}} tokens to {{ph.vqav2.grpor.r}}% at {{ph.vqav2.grpor.tok}} tokens, the level of Instruct's short answers ({{ph.vqav2.base.s}}%). The harness separates the two readings: the exact-match gain of {{ph.vqav2.gain}} points is {{ph.vqav2.audgain}} under the audited judge.

Short-prompt RL meets SFT on this data rather than passing it, {{ph.vqav2.grpo.s}}% against {{ph.vqav2.sft.s}}% on VQAv2, and it narrows what the model can sample: pass@32 falls from {{hk.base.vqav2.32}}% to {{hk.grpo.vqav2.32}}% while SFT raises it to {{hk.sft.vqav2.32}}%.

5Where the answer is out of reach, SFT goes first

On medical images SFT beats RL from Instruct in domain, and GRPO started from SFT is the best model in all three settings. SFT teaches the domain's answers; RL then polishes them, adding {{med.slk2.sftgrpo_sft.v}} points on SLAKE at 2B.

Reach shows why. On SLAKE's open-answer questions Instruct's pass@8, the share of questions where one of GRPO's eight samples is right, is {{reach.slk2.reach}}%. GRPO ends close to it at {{reach.slk2.rl}}% and SFT passes it at {{reach.slk2.sft}}%; 8B repeats this ({{reach.slk8.reach}}, {{reach.slk8.rl}}, {{reach.slk8.sft}}). Across {{reach.sets}} open-answer rows RL from Instruct ends at most {{reach.rl.most}} points above reach, and only on SLAKE does SFT pass it. RL can only reward answers the model samples; SFT can teach the others. This is an observed pattern with an untested explanation, in the spirit of what Yue et al. (2025) report for text; here it is measured per domain.

6What RL's gain is made of

Replacing each image with a black one shows what a model answers without looking. RL from Instruct answers as many questions blind as Instruct in {{ba.rl.level}} of {{ba.rl.settings}} settings, every interval including zero, and {{ba.rl.most}} points in the other. SFT answers more without the image in every setting it trained in. At 8B, {{ba.slk8.sft.blackgain}} of SFT's {{ba.slk8.sft.gain}}-point gain on SLAKE survives a black image, against {{ba.slk8.grpo.blackgain}} of RL's {{ba.slk8.grpo.gain}}. RL improves the answers that depend on the image; SFT also learns which answers are likely for a question.

On medical data RL also moves the model far less: its forward KL from Instruct is {{kl.med.lo}} to {{kl.med.hi}} times smaller than SFT's. RL's Razor (Shenfeld et al., 2025) finds that forgetting tracks this distance, and GRPO loses less than SFT on PathVQA in every setting. On photos and short-answer charts, where SFT's labels are close to what Instruct already says, both distances are small and SFT's is the smaller.

On sets no model trained on, RL keeps a small edge over SFT: {{tv.rl_sft}} points on TextVQA and {{vw.rl_sft}} on VizWiz. SFT on exactly the 4,000 questions GRPO drew does not close it.

Two limits go with these. In short-answer settings RL is overconfident, with a calibration error of {{cal.grpo.lo}}–{{cal.grpo.hi}} points against SFT's {{cal.sft.lo}}–{{cal.sft.hi}}. 7Why trust it: the harness

Nine layers, each answering one question about a result, each with its own measured number. Every difference reported here passes through all of them.

Inputs

Every dev and test image is checked against every training pool by a hash of its decoded pixels and dimensions, since the same image can carry different IDs in different sources.

8Open: one model for photos and charts

One model trained on photos and charts together lost its chart reasoning: it collapsed to {{ph.cq.mix.tok}}-token answers everywhere and ended at {{ph.cq.mix.r}}% on ChartQA against {{cq.grpo.r}}% for the chart-only model. It also trained for {{mix.updates}} updates against the chart-only model's {{cq.updates}}, and the collapse came between updates 1,750 and 2,000, so mixing and training length are confounded. A chart-only control at the same length is declared and running; it decides whether the fix is routing prompts by domain or knowing when to stop.

9Next

The same arms on a table grouped into general VQA, reasoning, document and chart, perception and hallucination benchmarks, with an image-gain column for each, plus one new training source each for reasoning and for documents. Predictions for the new sources are written before they train.
  2. Error analysis that names the errors. Rule-based checks on every evaluation and labeled samples at milestones, so each error type can be taken to the literature on reducing it.
  3. SFT on reasoning traces. The direct test of whether RL's chart gain comes from RL or from its reasoning targets.
  4. Medical imaging as its own milestone, from 2D medical questions to 3D abdominal CT.

Side note: do 3D CT benchmarks need the scan?

The same harness asks how much of two CT benchmarks is reachable without looking. On DeepTumorVQA a text-only rule scores {{au.mc.rule}}% against {{au.mc.chance}}% chance, and M3D-VQA's correct option is A in nearly half of its questions. A debiased DeepTumorVQA built here brings the rule to {{au.x.rule}}%, and on it GRPO and SFT reach the same accuracy. Full note.

AWhat did not work, and details

Each tried change, with what was expected and what it implies, is in the negatives checklist: on-policy distillation, filtered pools, refilling dead groups, a format reward, a perception loss, vision LoRA, upscaling, and {{knob.count}} training settings on ChartQA that read {{knob.lo}} to {{knob.hi}} points against a same-seed repeat of 0.5.

Scope

  • One training seed per model; intervals cover the sampling of test questions.
  • The judge audit, pass@k, the unseen sets and the CT audit are at 2B; the main photo matrix and SLAKE also at 8B.
  • Fixed test subsets scored locally, not benchmark evaluation servers; contamination is checked with exact image hashes against this project's training pools.

Reproducibility

Training uses TRL 1.8 with PEFT and colocated vLLM; evaluation runs through one protocol-hashed harness that stores per-question predictions for every model and protocol. Experiments are declared in experiments.toml before they run, and this page is rebuilt from the records with python -m scripts.report_page. About 279 GPU-hours in total, on cluster GPUs (RTX PRO 6000 96 GB, A100 80 GB, A30 24 GB) and one RTX 4090 24 GB; the 2B pipeline runs end to end on a single 24 GB GPU.

Terms

GRPO
Group Relative Policy Optimization: sample several responses per question and use each one's reward minus the group mean as its advantage, so no value network is needed.
Verifiable reward
A reward computed by a program, here 1 when the tagged answer matches the reference after normalization and 0 otherwise.
Audited judge
Exact-match hits plus LLM-judge acceptances, each acceptance weighted by the share of its stratum that an image-aware auditor confirmed.
pass@k
The probability that at least one of k samples is correct, estimated without bias from the samples drawn.
Reach
Instruct's pass@8 on open-answer questions: what the starting model can already sample within one GRPO group.
Forward KL
E[log p₀(y|x) − log p(y|x)] over responses y sampled from the starting model p₀: how much less likely the trained model p finds what the start would say.
Calibration error
The gap between confidence (share of samples giving the majority answer) and accuracy, averaged over 10 confidence bins.
